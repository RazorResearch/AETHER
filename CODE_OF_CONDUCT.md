# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

AETHER is a research project about measuring trade-offs honestly. We want the
same honesty in how people treat each other: a space where contributors of any
background, experience level or discipline can report a problem, propose an idea
and be reviewed on the merit of the work.

Everyone participating in this project — issues, pull requests, discussions,
reviews, documentation and any other project space — is expected to follow this
charter.

---

## Expected behaviour

- **Be precise.** Describe what you observed, on which version, and what you
  expected instead. "It feels slow" is not a bug report; "the Models table
  reflows at 1100 px" is.
- **Assume competence and good faith.** Ask a question before assuming a mistake.
- **Give reviewable feedback.** Point at the code, the behaviour or the claim —
  not at the person.
- **Respect scope.** AETHER deliberately does not ship an optimization engine
  yet. Refusals to fake capability are design decisions, not defects.
- **Credit work.** Reference prior art, datasets, models and tools, including
  their licenses.
- **Protect privacy.** Do not paste other people's paths, model files, logs or
  personal information into an issue.
- **Accept correction.** If a maintainer explains that a change conflicts with
  the project's scientific rules, adjust the change.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, threats, insults, sustained disruption, or personal attacks.
- Discriminatory or sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome attention.
- Publishing someone's private information without explicit permission.
- Deliberate misrepresentation of data, results, benchmarks or authorship —
  including presenting demonstration data as measured performance.
- Plagiarism, or contributing code or content you do not have the right to submit.
- Spam, undisclosed commercial promotion, or repeatedly reopening rejected
  decisions without new evidence.
- Retaliation against anyone who reports a concern in good faith.

---

## Scope

This charter applies to all project spaces (repository, issues, pull requests,
reviews, discussions) and whenever an individual is representing AETHER or
RazorResearch in public.

---

## Reporting a concern

- For conduct issues, contact the maintainers privately through GitHub (see the
  maintainer list in [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md)). Do not open a public issue
  naming the people involved.
- For security vulnerabilities, follow [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) — never a public
  issue.
- Include what happened, where, when, and any links or screenshots. Reports are
  handled confidentially; the reporter's identity is shared only with the people
  needed to resolve the case.

Maintainers are not neutral parties in a report that concerns them; if the report
involves a maintainer, send it to another maintainer.

---

## Enforcement

Responses are proportional, documented internally, and applied consistently:

1. **Correction** — a private note explaining the problem and what is expected.
2. **Warning** — a formal warning, with conditions for continued participation.
3. **Temporary restriction** — time-limited removal from interactions (issues,
   reviews, discussions) for repeated or serious behaviour.
4. **Permanent exclusion** — for severe violations or a pattern that persists
   after warnings.

Immediate exclusion may be applied for threats, harassment of a vulnerable
person, or publication of private information. Enforcement decisions may be
appealed once, in writing, to a maintainer not involved in the original decision.

---

## Good-faith disagreement is not a violation

Scientific debate — including firm disagreement about methodology, priorities or
design — is welcome. This charter targets conduct, not opinions. Refusing a pull
request, or stating that a claim is unsupported, is not harassment.
